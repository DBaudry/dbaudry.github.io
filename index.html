Reinforcement learning & online learning

Dorian Baudry

Research scientist · Inria, Grenoble

I am a research scientist (chargé de recherche) at Inria, in the GHOST team in Grenoble, affiliated with Université Grenoble Alpes.

My research asks how learning algorithms should explore and when gradient-based methods learn efficiently. I work on the theory of reinforcement learning, with connections to multi-armed bandits, online optimization and stochastic approximation.

I also supervise PhD students and research interns on these topics.

Research directions

Exploration, optimization and adaptation in sequential decision-making.

01

Exploration and posterior sampling

How should an algorithm explore?

I study randomized exploration methods that adapt to the difficulty of a learning problem, from non-parametric bandits to reinforcement learning.

Explore this direction ↗: Exploration and posterior sampling
02

Policy gradients and stochastic approximation

When do gradient-based methods learn efficiently?

I analyze how learning rates, noise and coupled updates affect the convergence, regret and bias of gradient-based learning algorithms.

Explore this direction ↗: Policy gradients and stochastic approximation
03

Adaptive and structured online learning

How can learning adapt to its environment?

I design online learning algorithms that exploit structure and account for changing environments, limited feedback and constraints.

Explore this direction ↗: Adaptive and structured online learning

Towards instance-dependent regret optimality in Episodic MDPs with Posterior Sampling

NeurIPS 2026

We separate the decision to explore from the choice of an exploration policy in posterior-sampling RL, taking a step toward instance-dependent regret optimality.

Victor Boone, Cyrille Kone, Waris Radji, Odalric-Ambrym Maillard, Dorian Baudry

The Bias of Nonlinear Two-Time-scale Stochastic Approximation under Constant Step-Sizes

Preprint · 2026

We bound the finite-time error and bias of coupled stochastic-approximation algorithms, showing how nonlinear dynamics and the two step sizes interact.

A Perturbation Approach to Unconstrained Linear Bandits

ICML 2026

We show how a perturbation scheme connects unconstrained linear bandits to online linear optimization, yielding guarantees for fixed and changing comparators.

Andrew Jacobsen, Dorian Baudry, Shinji Ito, Nicolò Cesa-Bianchi

Does Stochastic Gradient really succeed for bandits?

NeurIPS 2025 Oral

We characterize how the learning rate determines whether stochastic-gradient bandits achieve logarithmic regret or can suffer polynomial regret.
