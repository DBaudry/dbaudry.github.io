Before joining Inria in Grenoble, I was a postdoctoral researcher in the Department of Statistics at the University of Oxford, working with [Patrick Rebeschini](https://www.stats.ox.ac.uk/~rebeschi/). Patrick sparked my interest in policy gradient methods, and our collaboration led to my first paper on the subject, [Does Stochastic Gradient really succeed for bandits?](/publication/sgb/), presented as an oral at NeurIPS 2025. I also worked on online convex optimization, including learning in non-stationary environments.

Earlier, I worked with [Vianney Perchet](https://vianney.ai/) in the [Inria Fairplay team](https://team.inria.fr/fairplay/), based at ENSAE Paris. The team's energy and breadth of interests made this a particularly stimulating period, with projects at the intersection of machine learning, economics and computer science. My work ranged from structured and constrained bandits to [online auctions](/publication/auctions/), optimal stopping and reward lookahead in reinforcement learning.

I received my PhD in Computer Science from the University of Lille, supervised by [Emilie Kaufmann](https://chercheurs.lille.inria.fr/ekaufman/) and [Odalric-Ambrym Maillard](https://odalric-ambrymmaillard.github.io/) in the [Inria ScooL team](https://team.inria.fr/scool/). It was there that I discovered the broader field of reinforcement learning and developed my interest in the theory of sequential decision-making. My thesis focused on non-parametric algorithms for multi-armed bandits, with applications in agriculture and extensions to risk-aware learning and non-stationary environments.

Research visits have also been important in shaping my work. During my PhD, I spent three months at Kyoto University collaborating with [Junya Honda](https://stat.sys.i.kyoto-u.ac.jp/honda/index.html). In October 2025, I visited Nicolò Cesa-Bianchi in Milan, where I worked with him and Andrew Jacobsen on bandit convex optimization. This collaboration led to [A Perturbation Approach to Unconstrained Linear Bandits](/publication/unconstrained-linear-bandits/), presented at ICML 2026.
